# 🚕 Ola — Product Discovery Case Study

## 📌 Overview

This case study explores the Ola ride-booking experience from a **Product Management perspective**.

Instead of jumping directly to solutions, I approached the product by first understanding the **users, business model, user journey, potential pain points, and opportunities for improvement**.

The goal was to practice thinking like a Product Manager — **identify the right problem before designing the solution.**

---

## 🎯 Objective

The objective of this case study was to:

* Understand the Ola product and its users
* Explore how the business creates and captures value
* Identify potential user problems and friction points
* Develop problem hypotheses based on observations
* Prioritize opportunities
* Define a clear product goal
* Think about possible improvements from a user-centric perspective

---

## 🔍 My Product Discovery Approach

I followed a structured discovery process:

**1. Understand the Product**
↓
**2. Understand the Business**
↓
**3. Identify Users & Their Needs**
↓
**4. Map the User Journey**
↓
**5. Identify Potential Problems**
↓
**6. Create Problem Hypotheses**
↓
**7. Define the Product Goal**
↓
**8. Explore Potential Solutions**

This helped me avoid the common mistake of **starting with a solution before understanding the problem.**

---

## 👥 Users

I considered different stakeholders in the Ola ecosystem, including:

* Riders / Customers
* Drivers
* Ola
* Supporting ecosystem partners

Each stakeholder has different goals, expectations, and pain points, which makes the product more complex than simply "booking a cab."

---

## 🧩 Problem Discovery

During the discovery process, I looked at different stages of the ride-booking journey:

**Before Booking → Booking → Driver Matching → Pickup → Ride → Payment → Post-Ride**

At each stage, I considered:

* What is the user trying to accomplish?
* What could go wrong?
* Where might the user experience friction?
* What could cause frustration or drop-off?
* What business impact could the problem create?

The problems identified at this stage are treated as **hypotheses**, not assumptions of fact. They would need to be validated through user research and product data.

---

## 💡 Product Thinking

One of my key learnings from this exercise was:

> **A Product Manager's job is not simply to build features. It is to understand problems, validate them, prioritize them, and then determine whether a solution is worth building.**

This case study helped me practice moving from:

**Observation → Problem → Hypothesis → Validation → Opportunity → Solution**

rather than:

**Idea → Feature → Build**

## 🧠 Key Learnings

### 1. Start with the problem

A good product solution starts with a clearly defined problem.

### 2. Separate assumptions from evidence

Not every observed inconvenience is automatically a validated problem.

### 3. Think about multiple stakeholders

Improving the experience for one user group can sometimes create trade-offs for another.

### 4. Prioritization matters

A product team cannot solve every problem. Problems need to be evaluated based on factors such as **user impact, business impact, frequency, and feasibility.**

### 5. Discovery comes before delivery

Before thinking about *how to build something*, a PM needs to understand *why it should be built*.

## 🚀 Next Steps

If this were taken further as a real product discovery exercise, I would:

1. Conduct user interviews
2. Collect quantitative product data
3. Validate the identified problem hypotheses
4. Measure the frequency and severity of the problems
5. Prioritize the validated opportunity
6. Define success metrics
7. Develop and test a prototype
8. Run usability testing
9. Iterate based on user feedback
